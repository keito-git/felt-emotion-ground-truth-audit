# S2. Verbatim quotations from primary documents

This file records the statements on which the coding of the datasets discussed in the manuscript
rests. Each entry gives the dataset, the coded decision and the quotation as it appears in the
primary document. Quotations are reproduced from the text layer of the published documents.

## Datasets in which a record from the person exists and observers supply the ground truth

**IEMOCAP** — agreement type; validation self -> observer.
> "This self emotional evaluation was performed only in the spontaneous/unscripted scenarios"
> "Table VII compares the self-evaluation ("self") results with the assessment obtained from the rest of the evaluators ("others"). For this table, the emotional labels obtained from majority voting are assumed as ground truth. The turns in which the evaluators did not reach agreement were not considered."

**SEND** — agreement type; ground truth weighted by correlation with the observer average.
> "To calculate the "gold-standard" valence labels, we used the Evaluator Weighted Estimator (EWE), which provides an elegant formulation for weighting each observer's ratings by how well they correlate with the (unweighted) average of the ratings."

**TEC** — writers' own hashtags validated against trained judges.
> "We conduct experiments to show that the self-labeled hashtag annotations are consistent and match with the annotations of trained judges."
> "It is also a validation of the premise that the self-labeled emotion hashtags are consistent, at least to some degree, with the emotion labels given by trained human judges."

**JAFFE** — the posers' intended expression is set aside in favour of viewer ratings.
> "Ten expressers posed 3 or 4 examples of each of the six basic facial expressions ... for a total of 219 images"
> "we do not have to use the expression labels attached to each image (the emotion posed by the expresser) when comparing the model with the data. Instead, viewers rate the emotional content of each image using emotion adjectives."

**CK+** — the requested emotion is judged invalid and replaced by coder-validated labels.
> "While AU codes are well validated, emotion labels are not, as they refer to what was requested rather than what was actually performed"
> "The target expression for each sequence is fully FACS coded and emotion labels have been revised and validated."

## Datasets in which the person's report is demoted to a manipulation check

**WESAD** — the section titled "Obtaining Ground Truth" describes validation of the protocol.
> "In order to validate the study protocol, we collected five self-reports of each participant"

**BP4D** — self-report verifies the elicitation rather than defining the label.
> "The effectiveness of the eliciting methods has been verified by subject self-report and FACS analysis."

**RECOLA** — both views are collected; the released affective labels come from six annotators.
> "In addition to these recordings, 6 annotators measured emotion continuously on two dimensions: arousal and valence"
> "detailed annotation of emotional ... primitives evaluated from both internal and external views, i.e., from participants and annotators, respectively"

## Datasets in which the first-person record is excluded by design

**EmoBank** — both perspectives are enacted by annotators, and personal feeling is excluded for quality control.
> "each sentence was rated according to both the (perceived) writer and reader emotion (henceforth, WRITER and READER)"
> "for the reader's perspective, we asked what emotion would be evoked in an average reader (rather than asking for the rater's personal feelings). Both adjustments were made to establish more objective criteria for the exclusion of untrustworthy workers."
> "We find evidence for the supremacy of the reader's perspective in terms of IAA and rating intensity."

**SAMM** — self-reports are deliberately not collected.
> "tailor each video stimuli to each participant, rather than obtaining self-reports after the experiment"

**FER2013** — the label originates in the collection procedure.
> "The dataset was created using the Google image search API to search for images of faces that match a set of 184 emotion-related keywords"
> "The labels were intended to resemble those in the training set."

**EmoInt** — annotators are asked to infer what the speaker feels.
> "annotators (and automatic algorithms) are to infer, based on the tweet, the extent to which the speaker is likely feeling a particular emotion"

**ICT-MMMO** — the declared target is the speaker's expressed sentiment, and coders assign it.
> "the sentiment expressed by the person being videotaped. To achieve this goal, coders watched all of the review videos, and we instructed them to assign one label per movie review video."

## Datasets in which the person supplies the ground truth

**DEAP** — participants' self-assessments are the criterion; observers only select stimuli.
> "For self-assessment along these scales, we use the well-known self-assessment manikins (SAM)"
> "the final selection of 40 videos used in the experiment was made on the basis of subjective ratings by volunteers"

**MAHNOB-HCI** — participants report their felt emotions.
> "they watched 20 emotional videos and self-reported their felt emotions using arousal, valence, dominance, and predictability as well as emotional keywords"

**ASCERTAIN** — participants report after each clip.
> "After viewing each clip, users self-reported their emotional state in the form of affective ratings"

## Datasets whose declared target is the perception of others

**CMU-MOSEI** — the target is explicitly the perception of the crowd.
> "The annotation was carried out by 3 crowdsourced judges from Amazon Mechanical Turk platform. To avert implicitly biasing the judges and to capture the raw perception of the crowd, we avoided extensive annotation training"

**VAD** — the annotation is stated to derive from annotators' perception.
> "the annotation of our VAD dataset is derived from the annotators' perception and judgment of the video content"

**RAF-DB** — common expression perception is the object of study.
> "we propose to study the common expression perception by a reliable crowdsourcing approach"

**SEMAINE** — the continuous rating asks how the person appears to feel.
> "It allows raters to annotate material in terms of two long-established emotion dimensions, valence (how positive or negative the person appears to feel)"

## Datasets that withhold the decision or justify the substitution

**K-EmoCon** — the epistemic question is raised explicitly and three perspectives are released. This
resource lies outside the sampling frame and is discussed only as an illustrative case.
> "there may not be a ground truth for emotions. Should we consider what is most agreed upon by external observers of emotions as the ground truth, or what the person who experiences emotions reports to have felt the ground truth?"

**NNIME** — four perspectives are released together.
> "These emotion annotations include a diverse perspectives: peer-report, director-report, self-report, and observer-report."

**AMIGOS** — external ratings are justified by their correlation with the person's ratings.
> "We show that there is significant correlation between the internal and external annotation of valence and arousal for the short videos experiment, which indicates that external annotation is a good predictor of the affective state of participants."

**CASME II** — the person's report is one input among three.
> "In CASME II database, the micro-expressions are labeled based on the AUs, participants' self-report, and the contents of the video episodes."

## Other coded datasets

For the remaining datasets of the frame, the coded fields in S1 record the located statement in
summary form together with the decision. The quotations above cover every dataset that is named in
